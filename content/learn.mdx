# How to start — 2–4 week learning path

Audience: curious high-school / early undergrad scientists & engineers.

Prefer the illustrated **[Beginners track](/beginners)** (Ch.1–5) if you want pictures and video embeds first; use this page as the week-by-week checklist.  
Assumption: ~5–8 hours/week. Mix reading, media, and safe experiments.

<Callout type="info">
This is a **frontier field** — expect demos and principles more than consumer products.
</Callout>

## Week 1 — Big picture & vocabulary

**Goals:** Define self-assembly; static vs dynamic; scales from molecules to robots.

| Day | Activity | Source |
|-----|----------|--------|

**Checkpoint:** Explain self-assembly to a friend in &lt;2 minutes without jargon.

## Week 4 — Mini-project & share

**Goals:** Synthesize; produce something this site could feature (student work).

**Pick one mini-project:**
1. **Sim showcase:** NetLogo/Python swarm or tile assembler with a README + short screen recording.
2. **Hardware toy:** Magnet floatiles or patchy 3D-printed modules with a photo lab report (Exps 1 or 7).
3. **Explainers:** 800–1000 word blog for peers: “DNA origami in plain language” or “What Kilobots taught us about swarms,” citing ≥3 corpus papers.
4. **Curriculum card:** Design a 45-minute workshop for younger students using only Exp 2–4 materials.

**Deliverables checklist:**
- [ ] Hypothesis / question
- [ ] Methods someone else can repeat
- [ ] Data (table or figures)
- [ ] Link to ≥2 papers from [Papers](/papers)
- [ ] “What I’d do next” + one safety note

## Stretch (optional, weeks 3–4+)

- Evans et al. 2024 multifarious assembly (conceptually; don’t attempt wet lab).
- Particle robotics Nature 2019 (alternative swarm philosophy).
- DNA bricks Ke et al. 2012 vs origami comparison chart.

## Recommended weekly rhythm

1. **Read** (90 min) → 2. **Watch** (30–45 min) → 3. **Do** (2–3 h experiment/sim) → 4. **Write** (45 min notes).

Avoid binge-reading paywalled PDFs; prefer open PDFs/arXiv + figure study.

## Prerequisites & tools

- Comfortable with algebra-level graphs; optional intro programming for sims.
- Free tools: Python, NetLogo, cadnano (if available), phone camera, kitchen tray experiments.
- Mentors: science teacher / maker-space for 3D printing magnets safely.

## Outcomes after 2–4 weeks

- Navigate DNA vs robotics branches of self-assembly.
- Name landmark papers and what each contributed.
- Run a safe physical or simulated assembly experiment with measurements.
- Explain *why* programmable matter and molecular robots are mostly still research.
