# Swarm robotics & programmable matter

From Claytronics vision to Kilobots, modular reconfigurable robots, particle robots, and 2024 surveys.

<Callout type="warning">
The programmable-matter north star (millions of modules reshaping 3D objects on demand) is **still largely unrealized**. Treat demos as proofs of principle, not product catalogs.
</Callout>

## 1. Vision — Programmable Matter (Goldstein et al. 2005)

**Goldstein, Campbell, Mowry**, *Programmable Matter*, *IEEE Computer*  
[DOI](https://doi.org/10.1109/MC.2005.198) · [Project page](http://www.cs.cmu.edu/~claytronics/papers/goldstein-computer05.html)

- Ensembles of tiny robots (“**catoms**”) form and reshape 3D objects.
- Goal is synthetic reality: physical artifacts that mimic shape, motion, appearance, touch — not teleportation of atoms.
- Hardware, algorithms, and power are co-equal challenges.
- Hub: [CMU Claytronics](http://www.cs.cmu.edu/~claytronics/index.html)

**Beginner takeaway:** Clear, honest vision piece — aspirational north star.

## 2. Visible algorithmic self-assembly — Kilobots (2014)

**Rubenstein, Cornejo, Nagpal**, *Programmable self-assembly in a thousand-robot swarm*, *Science*  
[DOI](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1254295) · [Harvard news](https://seas.harvard.edu/news/self-organizing-thousand-robot-swarm)

- **1,024 Kilobots** form programmed 2D shapes using only local communication.
- Robots are simple, noisy, unreliable — collective algorithms still work.
- Seed robots define a coordinate origin; others edge-follow and fill the shape.
- Separates “smart global result” from “dumb local rules.”

**Platform paper:** Rubenstein, Ahler, Nagpal — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.robot.2013.08.006)  
Designed for hundreds–thousands of units (~\$14 parts originally); vibration locomotion + IR messaging.

**Watch:** [Thousand-Kilobot demo](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xK54Bu9HFRw) · [Intro to Kilobot](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ISMwLCFwgK4)

## 3. Modular reconfigurable robotics — Seo, Paik, Yim (2019)

**Seo, Paik, Yim**, *Modular Reconfigurable Robotics*, *Annu. Rev. Control Robot. Auton. Syst.*  
[DOI](https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev-control-053018-023834)

- Taxonomy: lattice, chain, mobile, hybrid.
- Promise: versatility, robustness, potentially lower cost vs fixed morphology.
- Hardware (connectors, actuators) and software (planning, control) are equally hard.
- Open bar: “Can it form a wrench that turns a bolt?”

## 4. Algorithms for self-reconfiguration — Thalamy et al. (2019)

**Thalamy, Piranda, Bourgeois**, *A survey of autonomous self-reconfiguration methods…*, *Robotics and Autonomous Systems*  
[DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.robot.2019.07.012)

- Algorithm-first companion to hardware surveys.
- Current algorithms are far from visionary requirements (huge module counts, speed, reliability).
- Emphasizes distributed methods that scale.

## 5. Another philosophy — Particle robotics (2019)

**Li, Batra, Brown, et al. (incl. Rus, Lipson)**, *Particle robotics…*, *Nature*  
[DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-019-1022-9) · [Lab page](https://www.creativemachineslab.com/particle-robotics.html)

- Loosely coupled units that only expand/contract.
- Collective locomotion emerges from statistical-mechanics-style coordination.
- Works even with ~20% malfunctioning particles in demos.
- Different from precise docking modular robots — embrace disorder + coupling.

## DNA sticky ends vs robot local messages

| | DNA sticky ends | Robot local messages |
|---|-----------------|----------------------|
| “Glue” | Sequence complementarity | IR / radio / contact |
| Scale | Nanometers | Centimeters+ |
| Failure mode | Misfolds, kinetic traps | Noise, localization error, battery |
| Alike | Local rules → global structure; seeds matter; errors are first-class |
