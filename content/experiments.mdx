# Safe starter experiments

All activities avoid hazardous chemistry, open flames for chemistry, high-voltage, pressurized gases, and biological agents. Suitable for young scientists/engineers with adult supervision where noted. Prefer simulation when wet/physical resources are limited.

**Safety baseline:** no strong acids/bases, no organic solvents beyond household vinegar/soap, no neodymium magnet swallowing risk for young children (use taped/embedded magnets), eye protection for any small-parts / elastic-band work.

<Callout type="info">
**Start here labs:** Experiments **1–5** are the best first week. Experiments **6–10** deepen theory, geometry, programmable-matter thinking, and DNA design (CAD only).
</Callout>

## Experiment 1 — Floating magnet tiles (2D magnetic self-assembly)

**Goal:** Watch discrete parts form ordered clusters from local attraction/repulsion rules.

**Materials:** Foam or cork tiles; small disc magnets (tape/embed so they cannot detach); shallow tray of water; ruler; phone camera. Optional: orbital shaker or gentle hand agitation.

**Steps:**
1. Embed magnets with known pole orientations (e.g., N-up vs S-up patterns, or edge magnets with assigned polarity).
2. Float 8–20 tiles on water; record initial disordered state.
3. Agitate gently for 30–60 s; stop; photograph.
4. Repeat at “low / medium / high” agitation (proxy for temperature/noise).

**Measurement:** Cluster size distribution; number of distinct motifs; time to first stable dimer/trimer.

**Success criteria:** At least one reproducible ordered motif appears more often than chance across 5 trials.

**Discussion:** Which rules (polarity patterns) favor chains vs closed shapes? How does excess shaking cause defects?

## Experiment 2 — Cheerios / capillary “FloaTiles” effect

**Goal:** Observe capillary-driven assembly without magnets.

**Materials:** Bowl of water; Cheerios or 3D-printed/light floating shapes; dish soap (for a disruption trial); camera.

**Steps:**
1. Sprinkle pieces on still water; observe clustering at the surface.
2. Time how long until a connected cluster forms.
3. Add one drop of dish soap far from the cluster; observe change.

**Measurement:** Cluster diameter vs time; effect of soap (surface-tension change).

**Success criteria:** Clear before/after difference when surface tension is disrupted.

**Discussion:** How is this like/unlike molecular hydrophobic assembly? Why is soap a “rule change”?

**Refs for educators:** [UCSD MRSEC experimental self-assembly](https://mrsec.ucsd.edu/experimental-self-assembly/); [FloaTiles](https://karegeo.github.io/floatiles/)

## Experiment 3 — Soap-bubble raft packing

**Goal:** See emergent crystal-like order from identical “particles.”

**Materials:** Dish soap + water + glycerin (optional); shallow tray; straw; phone camera; graph paper overlay.

**Steps:**
1. Blow a monolayer of similar-sized bubbles.
2. Photograph packing; count neighbors (coordination number).
3. Intentionally introduce a larger/smaller bubble; watch defect formation.

**Measurement:** Fraction of 6-neighbor bubbles; defect count.

**Success criteria:** Majority hexagonal packing in a calm raft; visible defects around size impurities.

**Discussion:** Polydispersity and defects — why monodisperse colloids matter in labs.

## Experiment 4 — Paperclip / chain “foldamer” with local rules (tabletop algorithm)

**Goal:** Simulate algorithmic assembly with physical tokens (no chemistry).

**Materials:** Colored paperclips or LEGO bricks with labeled edges (A/a, B/b); printed rule card; timer.

**Steps:**
1. Assign complementary edges (A sticks only to a, etc.).
2. Start from a “seed” piece; add pieces only when a rule matches (cooperative binding optional: need 2 matching edges).
3. Compare greedy random attachment vs rule-constrained attachment.

**Measurement:** Yield of target shape over 10 runs; number of trapped wrong assemblies.

**Success criteria:** Rule-constrained protocol reaches target ≥70% of runs; random does worse.

**Discussion:** Connect to DNA sticky ends and aTAM “temperature” (how many bonds needed).

## Experiment 5 — Kilobot / swarm simulation lab (software)

**Goal:** Recreate shape formation with local rules in simulation.

**Materials:** Laptop; Kilombo / ARGoS / NetLogo / Python agent-based sim (pick one available); target shape bitmap.

**Steps:**
1. Implement robots with: edge-following, distance-to-seed estimate, join-if-inside-shape.
2. Run N = 50, 200, 500 agents; inject 5–10% “failed” robots.

**Measurement:** Fraction of shape filled; completion time; defect holes.

**Success criteria:** Shape recognizable at N≥200 with ≤10% failures still completing outline.

**Discussion:** Why local communication scales; what breaks in cluttered environments.

**Media tie-in:** [Harvard Kilobot swarm video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xK54Bu9HFRw)

## Experiment 6 — Cellular automaton / tile assembly toy model

**Goal:** Connect self-assembly to computation.

**Materials:** Computer; Python or online CA tool; optional physical tiles printed on cardstock.

**Steps:**
1. Code a 1D or 2D tile assembly with glue strengths 0/1/2 and a temperature threshold.
2. Grow from a seed; try an XOR or binary-counter tile set (simplified).
3. Randomly flip glue matches with probability p (errors).

**Measurement:** Correct structure length/area vs p.

**Success criteria:** Plot showing sharp drop in yield as p increases; write 1 paragraph relating to experimental DNA tile error.

**Discussion:** Why proofreading / redundancy appears in advanced designs.

## Experiment 7 — Patchy-particle packing with 3D-printed modules (dry)

**Goal:** Explore geometry-directed assembly.

**Materials:** 3D-printed tetrahedra/cubes with Velcro or embedded magnets on chosen faces; tray; optional mild vibration plate.

**Steps:**
1. Design two species: “only face X binds.”
2. Shake gently in a box; separate bound dimers/trimers.
3. Compare isotropic Velcro-all-faces vs patchy designs.

**Measurement:** Fraction of intended bonds vs unintended.

**Success criteria:** Patchy design shows ≥2× higher specificity than isotropic control.

**Discussion:** Directional interactions in DNA origami / colloidal patchy particles.

## Experiment 8 — “Programmable matter” reconfiguration on a grid

**Goal:** Experience distributed reconfiguration cost.

**Materials:** Checkerboard; stackable cubes or sliding pixels; printed start/goal shapes; stopwatch. Optional: micro:bit/LEGO robots pushing blocks.

**Steps:**
1. From shape A to shape B using only local moves (slide to empty neighbor; keep connectivity if required).
2. Compare centralized planner (one student directs) vs local rules (each cube follows a simple priority).

**Measurement:** Move count; time; connectivity violations.

**Success criteria:** Both methods reach goal; students quantify move overhead of local rules.

**Discussion:** Why programmable-matter algorithms care about connectivity and parallel moves.

## Experiment 9 — Evaporative coffee-ring / crystal demo (household, observational)

**Goal:** See nonequilibrium patterns from drying — contrast with designed assembly.

**Materials:** Coffee or food coloring in water; glass slide or white plate; lamp; camera.

**Steps:**
1. Place drops of different concentrations; let dry undisturbed.
2. Photograph rings/patterns; vary humidity/airflow gently with a fan at low setting.

**Measurement:** Ring width vs concentration (qualitative OK).

**Success criteria:** Reproducible coffee-ring observation; written contrast vs “programmed” sticky-end assembly.

**Discussion:** Not all self-organization is useful/designed — kinetics and evaporation create patterns “for free.”

**Safety:** Food-safe materials only; no solvents.

## Experiment 10 — DNA origami *design* assignment (simulation / CAD only)

**Goal:** Learn scaffolded origami workflow without wet lab.

**Materials:** Computer; cadnano / oxDNA / online DNA design tutorials; Rothemund 2006 figures ([author PDF](https://www.dna.caltech.edu/Papers/DNAorigami-nature.pdf)).

**Steps:**
1. Sketch a simple 2D silhouette (heart, letter, triangle).
2. Route a scaffold conceptually (or in cadnano if available).
3. List staple roles; estimate approximate size (~100 nm classic origami).
4. Write a mini “methods” section: anneal, verify with AFM (describe, don’t perform if unequipped).

**Measurement:** Completeness of design checklist; peer review of staple/scaffold understanding.

**Success criteria:** Correct explanation of scaffold vs staples; scale bar estimate; one limitation noted (cost, yield, imaging).

**Discussion:** Why educational DNA wet labs are rare (cost, equipment) — simulation is a valid scientific skill.

**Safety:** No oligonucleotides ordered for young students unless under proper lab program.
