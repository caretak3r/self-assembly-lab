## Highlights

### 1. Spatially Resolved Nucleated Polymerization: A Free-Boundary Model of Protein Aggregation in Concentrated Solutions
- **Authors:** Pouria A. Mistani
- **Submitted:** 2026-09-23
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.27185
- **Why newsworthy:** Extends Lumry–Eyring nucleated polymerization so aggregates are spatial regions (level sets) with growth/condensation set by surface bonding sites. Collision efficiency and Fuchs stability ratio are computed from Wertheim theory, not fitted; well-mixed averages recover classic rate equations; many-body merge rates run 2.2–3.8× faster than two-body.
- **Beginner hook:** Protein clumps are growing blobs with surfaces that bump and fuse — modeling those surfaces connects math to real shapes.
- **Caveat:** Continuum theory / simulation; not a classroom wet lab.

### 2. Uphill and downhill first passage of an active Brownian particle: Asymmetry and exact path reweighting
- **Authors:** Mykola Tasinkevych, Xuan My Le, Artem Ryabov
- **Submitted:** 2026-09-23
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.27425
- **Why newsworthy:** Self-propulsion breaks the passive result that uphill and downhill first-passage *time* distributions match. An exact spatial-reflection reweighting still relates the two path ensembles and lets rare uphill statistics be reconstructed from common downhill trajectories.
- **Beginner hook:** Self-powered walkers take different kinds of paths against a push — a clever mirror trick still connects uphill and downhill.
- **Caveat:** Theory + simulation.

### 3. Insight into ordering at nematic twist-bend interfaces
- **Authors:** Szymon Drzazga, Piotr Kubala, Lech Longa
- **Submitted:** 2026-09-23
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.28012
- **Why newsworthy:** Monte Carlo + MD of repulsive bent particles: opposite-handedness twist-bend domains meet in a density-modulated splay-bend-twist interface (period ~½ bulk pitch) with an undulating zero-twist surface — not a gradual untwist.
- **Beginner hook:** Left- and right-corkscrew liquid-crystal domains do not shake hands smoothly — the border builds its own ripple pattern.
- **Caveat:** Simulation of repulsive bent rods.

### 4. Control of filament network rigidity by the condensation of crowding molecules
- **Authors:** Jiyong Cheon, Zhengyan Lin, Dean Kim, Anirudh Malli, Itamar Kolvin
- **Submitted:** 2026-09-22
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.26983
- **Why newsworthy:** Soft-matter experiment: dissolved crowders keep a helical-filament network rigid; when crowders condense into droplets, rigidity collapses by ~an order of magnitude and returns when condensates dissolve — osmotic-pressure tuning as a design principle.
- **Beginner hook:** Crowding molecules can act like invisible glue pressure — gather into droplets and the filament scaffolding goes limp, then stiffens again when remixed.
- **Caveat:** Lab filament + crowder gels; we do **not** propose classroom LLPS chemistry.

### 5. Active Self-Consistent Field Theory for Ornstein-Uhlenbeck Polymers
- **Authors:** Yuliang Huang, Chun-Lai Ren, Qiyun Tang
- **Submitted:** 2026-09-23
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.28124
- **Why newsworthy:** Active extension of polymer SCFT (χN + persistence time τ). Increasing activity suppresses microphase separation; density-modulation amplitude decays as Δφ ∝ τ^{-1/2} at large τ, independent of composition.
- **Beginner hook:** Classic block-copolymer stripe recipes assume sleepy chains — give them persistent kicks and the stripes fade on a clean power law.
- **Caveat:** Field theory / simulation.
