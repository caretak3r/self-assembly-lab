## Highlights

### 1. Phase diagram morphology shapes droplet propulsion in chemical gradients
- **Authors:** Stefan Köstler, Malcolm Steen, David Zwicker
- **Submitted:** 2026-09-21
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.24529
- **Why newsworthy:** Surfactant-free thin-interface theory: droplet velocity from size, viscosity, surface-tension sensitivity to a regulating component, and that component’s chemical-potential gradient. Droplets move toward regions of lower stability as encoded in phase-diagram morphology (especially near critical points); numerics confirm.
- **Beginner hook:** The environment’s phase diagram can steer a soft droplet — continuing yesterday’s “soft bag that swims” theme without needing a propeller or engineered surfactant.
- **Caveat:** Theory + simulation; not a classroom chemistry kit.

### 2. An active soft glassy rheology model
- **Authors:** Raffaele Mendozza, Tobias Müller, Peter Sollich
- **Submitted:** 2026-09-19
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.23168
- **Why newsworthy:** Soft Glassy Rheology extended with a persistent stochastic active strain rate on mesoscopic elements. Activity opens a long-time relaxation channel — crossover from SGR power-law rheology to Maxwell-like fluidisation — with a fluidisation timescale analogous to effective-temperature pictures of active matter.
- **Beginner hook:** Being “alive” inside a jammed soft material can unjam it on long timescales — relevant wherever self-assembly and collective motion live in dense soft media.
- **Caveat:** Mesoscopic theory/simulation.

### 3. Phase separation in a system of Brownian inertial rotors
- **Authors:** Lucio Mauro Carenza, Pasquale Digregorio, Massimiliano Semeraro, Antonio Suma, Ignacio Pagonabarraga, Giuseppe Gonnella
- **Submitted:** 2026-09-18
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.21556
- **Why newsworthy:** Torque-driven inertial dumbbells (chiral rotors) with hard-core repulsion only phase-separate into dense liquid + hexatic regions via an inertia-controlled hot-dilute / cool-dense mechanism; clusters spontaneously rigid-rotate about their centers of mass.
- **Beginner hook:** Spinning parts that only bounce off each other can still assemble rotating “machines” — chirality + inertia as knobs, no glue required.
- **Caveat:** Simulation only.

### 4. Transport of deformable vesicles driven by chiral active Brownian particles
- **Authors:** Dipak Patra, Anil Kumar Dasanna
- **Submitted:** 2026-09-16
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.18743
- **Why newsworthy:** Fixed-area vesicle filled with chiral active particles shows run-and-tumble-like migration, rotors, and persistent spinning. Vesicle rotation is non-monotonic in chirality — an optimal twist maximizes spin — with a scaling collapse across activity strengths.
- **Beginner hook:** Twist inside a soft bag can optimally spin or ferry the bag — same soft-boundary motif as rafts and phase-diagram droplets.
- **Caveat:** Simulation only.
