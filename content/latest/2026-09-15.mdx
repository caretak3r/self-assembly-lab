## Highlights

### 1. Assembly of Complex Colloidal Systems Using DNA (2024)
- **Authors:** William M. Jacobs, W. Benjamin Rogers
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.08988
- **Why newsworthy:** Comprehensive open review of DNA-programmed colloids: interactions, nucleation/growth kinetics, supracrystals, self-limiting assemblies, inverse design, multifarious and dissipative frontiers.
- **Beginner hook:** Explains why “just make complementary DNA” is not enough — temperature windows and kinetics dominate outcomes.
- **Open problems:** Pathway-aware inverse design; encoding multiple targets; nonequilibrium steady-state assembly.

### 2. Pattern recognition in the nucleation kinetics of non-equilibrium self-assembly (Nature 2024)
- **Authors:** Constantine G. Evans, Jackson O’Brien, Erik Winfree, Arvind Murugan
- **Link:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-023-06890-z · [Supp](https://www.dna.caltech.edu/SupplementaryMaterial/MultifariousSST/)
- **Why newsworthy:** Experimental multifarious DNA-tile system; concentration patterns select among shapes; nucleation as classifier.
- **Beginner hook:** One parts library, many programs — like software, but molecules.
- **Caveat:** Research-lab experiment (long anneals); not a classroom wet lab.

### 3. From Conventional to Programmable Matter Systems (ACM Computing Surveys 2024)
- **Authors:** Chafik, Gaber, Tayane, Ennaji, Bourgeois, El Ghazawi
- **Link:** https://doi.org/10.1145/3653671
- **Why newsworthy:** Broad 2024 survey of programmable-matter designs, materials, and tech stacks.
- **Beginner hook:** Updates the Claytronics-era vision with today’s fragmented landscape.
- **Open problems:** Energy, data, stimuli control, inconsistent device categories.

### 4. Decoding Modular Reconfigurable Robots (arXiv 2023 / IJRR 2024)
- **Links:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.09743 · https://doi.org/10.1177/02783649241283847
- **Why newsworthy:** Mechanism-and-design survey useful for comparing connectors/actuators.
- **Beginner hook:** Helps students reverse-engineer “how does this module move and latch?”

### 5. Dynamic DNA superstructures with emergent functions (Nanoscale Horizons 2025)
- **Authors:** Duke, Sterling, Teng, Altunina, Martynenko, Ke, Castro, Arya
- **Link:** https://doi.org/10.1039/D5NH00436E
- **Why newsworthy:** 2025 review merging dynamic DNA devices with hierarchical superstructures.
- **Beginner hook:** Next chapter after static origami sculptures.

### 6. Programming DNA machines to move (Nature Reviews Chemistry 2026)
- **Link:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41570-025-00791-7
- **Why newsworthy:** Metrics-focused review of DNA motors/walkers/switches vs biological motors.
- **Beginner hook:** Demystifies hype around “nanorobots.”
- **Note:** Published Jan 2026 — recent relative to this corpus date (Sep 2026).

### 7. Lipid vesicle-based molecular robots (Lab on a Chip 2024)
- **Link:** https://doi.org/10.1039/D3LC00860F
- **Why newsworthy:** Soft-matter / vesicle “body” framing for molecular robots.
- **Beginner hook:** Sensors + computers + actuators inside a lipid compartment — roadmap, not finished products.

### 8. Research on Reconfiguration Strategies for Self-reconfiguring Modular Robots (JIRS 2024)
- **Link:** https://doi.org/10.1007/s10846-024-02067-6
- **Why newsworthy:** 2024 review focused on reconfiguration *strategies* (grid/chain, matching, efficiency, energy).
- **Beginner hook:** Algorithm/strategy layer above hardware taxonomy.

### 9. ModCube / RS-ModCubes underwater modular robots (arXiv 2024)
- **Links:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.15627 · https://arxiv.org/html/2409.15627v2
- **Why newsworthy:** Concrete 2024 hardware demos of self-assembling/reconfigurable cubic underwater modules.
- **Beginner hook:** Shows modular self-assembly attempting real environments (underwater), not only desktop labs.
- **Caveat:** Early prototypes; treat as research demos.
