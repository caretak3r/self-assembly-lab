## Highlights

### 1. Self-limited stacking of non-Euclidean colloidal shells: From saddles to caps
- **Authors:** Kyle T. Sullivan, Mark J. Stevens, Gregory M. Grason
- **Submitted:** 2026-09-21
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.25399
- **Why newsworthy:** Geometric frustration in curved colloidal “curvamers” extended from cylinders to spherical caps and saddles. Stretching vs bending (FvK-like thickness measure) sets how fast elastic energy grows with stack height; typical self-limiting sizes largest for cylinders, smallest for caps, intermediate for saddles — with thin saddles giving especially robust size selection.
- **Beginner hook:** Curved pieces that won’t tile flat space can still assemble towers that stop at a designed height — curvature as a built-in ruler.
- **Caveat:** Continuum + coarse-grained simulation; not a classroom chemistry kit.

### 2. Dense packing of tetrahedra in cylinders
- **Authors:** Gabrielle N. Jones, Adil Mughal, Philipp W. A. Schönhöfer, Sharon C. Glotzer
- **Submitted:** 2026-09-22
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.26627
- **Why newsworthy:** First systematic densest-packings map of regular tetrahedra in a circular cylinder: chiral Tetra Helices vs achiral Dimer Chains. Boerdijk–Coxeter helix emerges naturally under confinement; at a critical diameter the two families coexist at identical packing fraction.
- **Beginner hook:** Tube diameter alone can choose spiral vs paired stacks of the same sharp pieces — packing as programmable geometry.
- **Caveat:** Simulation + analytic packing theory.

### 3. Statistical signatures of microorganism motility under environmental stress
- **Authors:** Luiza Treichel Mossi, Nicole Mazzitelli Narvaz, Larissa da Silva Bento, Roger Modena, Bernardo Boatini, Amalia Garcez, Leonardo Gregory Brunnet, Alexandre Arenzon, Carolina Brito
- **Submitted:** 2026-09-21
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.25403
- **Why newsworthy:** Video microscopy of *Paramecium* and *Artemia* under baseline vs stress; trajectory metrics + data-fit run-and-tumble-style models. Path statistics proposed as physiological-stress indicators — an accessible active-matter experiment.
- **Beginner hook:** Living microscopic swimmers are active matter you can track — change the environment carefully and the path statistics change.
- **Caveat:** Experimental organisms; any classroom live-culture work needs adult supervision and ethical handling (no hazardous chemistry).

### 4. Mesoscale heterogeneity in protein hydrogels from dynamic unfolding
- **Authors:** Victoria Byelova, Lorna Dougan, David Head
- **Submitted:** 2026-09-22
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.26681
- **Why newsworthy:** Coarse-grained chemically crosslinked protein gels with one-step unfolding produce crowded high-density clusters linked by low-density stranded corridors; post-gelation relaxation shifts fractal metrics more than unfolding-barrier height.
- **Beginner hook:** Soft gels are not uniform Jell-O — after they set, dense clumps remain linked by stringy bridges.
- **Caveat:** Simulation only.

### 5. Linear and nonlinear active microrheology via fluid particle dynamics
- **Authors:** Muhammed Muhsin Abdul Azeez, Henning Reinken, Andreas M. Menzel
- **Submitted:** 2026-09-22
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.26431
- **Why newsworthy:** Microrheology method extended to viscoelastic/elastic media and nonlinear drive. Large forcing yields distorted stress–strain loops and net probe drift (near walls or between two probes) that vanishes in purely viscous or purely elastic limits — tweezers/magnetic-drive analogue.
- **Beginner hook:** Continues yesterday’s jammed-soft-matter theme — push a tiny bead hard enough and viscoelastic gels drift in ways linear rheology misses.
- **Caveat:** Simulation.
