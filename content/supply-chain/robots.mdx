# Modular and swarm robots

Digests like swarm morphogenesis, robotic budding, and the self-folding CurveQuad robot are built mostly from **ordinary electronics and printed parts**. This is the most accessible domain to source.

## What exists today

- **Brains**: cheap microcontrollers (ESP32, Arduino-class boards, RP2040) with radio or infrared links.
- **Muscles**: small DC and vibration motors, micro servos, and shape-memory or magnetic actuators.
- **Docking**: neodymium magnets, pogo pins, latches, and printed snap fits.
- **Bodies**: FDM and resin 3D printing, laser-cut sheet, and flexible PCBs that fold into shape.
- **Boards**: rigid and flexible PCB prototyping is fast and global.

## Maturity

- Parts: **hobbyist** to **industrial**. Everything above ships to individuals in most countries.
- Swarm or modular robot *platforms*: mostly **university-lab** (open designs, research kits).
- Self-assembling or self-reproducing robot *products*: **not yet available**.

## Bottlenecks and gaps

- **Connectors** that are small, strong, self-aligning, and pass power and data are still custom for each lab.
- **Power**: batteries dominate size and weight at small scales, and charging hundreds of robots is a logistics problem.
- **Cost per unit**: a swarm of 1,000 needs each robot to be cheap, so hand assembly doesn't scale.
- **Shrinking below a centimeter** pushes you out of hobby parts and into [microfabrication](/supply-chain/microfab).

## What would need to exist for scale-up

Standard, open docking connectors; cheap contract assembly of identical small modules (the way PCB assembly services work now); swarm charging and programming racks; and design rules for folding flex circuits that any PCB house can follow.

## Safety notes

- Strong magnets pinch skin and are dangerous if swallowed. Keep them away from young children.
- Lithium batteries can catch fire. Use protected cells, a proper charger, and follow [shipping rules](https://www.phmsa.dot.gov/lithiumbatteries).
- Some radio modules and advanced electronics carry export rules. Hobby parts usually don't, but check before shipping abroad.
