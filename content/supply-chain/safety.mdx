# Safety and regulation

Sourcing isn't only about *where* to buy. It's also about **what rules come with it**. This page is a short, age-appropriate map, not legal advice. Your teacher, lab manager, or institution's safety office always has the final word.

| Area | What to know | Learn more |
|---|---|---|
| Nanomaterials | Free nanoparticles, especially dry powders, can be breathed in. Prefer liquid suspensions and work in a fume hood or enclosure | [NIOSH nanotechnology](https://www.cdc.gov/niosh/nano/about/index.html) |
| DNA synthesis | Suppliers screen orders and ask who you are. That's normal and good | [International Gene Synthesis Consortium](https://genesynthesisconsortium.org/) |
| Living cells and bacteria | Biosafety rules apply even to harmless strains | [NIH biosafety and biosecurity policies](https://osp.od.nih.gov/policies/biosafety-and-biosecurity-policy/) |
| Lithium batteries | Fire risk. Shipping is regulated as hazardous material | [PHMSA lithium battery rules](https://www.phmsa.dot.gov/lithiumbatteries) |
| Export controls | Some advanced fabrication tools, materials, and technical data need licenses to ship abroad | [US Bureau of Industry and Security](https://www.bis.gov/) |
| Magnets | Strong magnets pinch and are dangerous if swallowed | Manufacturer guidance, for example [K&J Magnetics](https://www.kjmagnetics.com/) |
| Cleanroom chemicals | Hydrofluoric acid and toxic gases are for trained users only | Your facility's training |

<Callout type="info">
If a supplier asks for an institutional address, a PI or teacher's name, or the purpose of your order, that is the system working as designed. Don't try to get around it. Find a mentor or program instead.
</Callout>

Outside the US, look for your country's equivalents: workplace safety agency, biosafety rules, and export-control authority.
