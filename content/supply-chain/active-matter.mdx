# Active matter

Active fluids, swimming droplets, and self-propelled particles in the digests need **parts that use energy to move**: motor proteins, catalytic particles, bacteria, or tiny vibrating robots.

## What exists today

- **Microtubule and kinesin proteins**: the classic recipe for "active nematic" fluids that churn on their own when fed ATP.
- **Catalytic Janus particles**: half-coated with platinum or similar so they push themselves through fuel. Mostly lab-made by coating half of a bead layer with metal.
- **Bacteria** as natural swimmers, which needs biology lab rules.
- **Macroscopic analogues**: bristlebots, vibrating tables, and camphor boats you can build at home.

## Maturity

- Macroscopic analogues: **hobbyist**.
- Protein-based and catalytic active matter: **university-lab**.
- Active-matter products (self-mixing fluids, self-healing materials): **not yet available**.

## Bottlenecks and gaps

- There is **no turnkey active-fluid kit**. Labs combine proteins, motor clusters, crowding agents, and ATP-regenerating systems by recipe.
- Proteins are **expensive, fragile, and need cold shipping**.
- Activity runs down as fuel is used up, so experiments last hours, not months.
- Catalytic Janus particles need a **metal evaporator** in a cleanroom.

## What would need to exist for scale-up

Stable, room-temperature active-fluid kits, longer-lived fuel systems, catalog Janus swimmers below 5 µm, and engineered materials that stay active for days.

## Safety notes

- Hydrogen peroxide fuel for catalytic swimmers is an irritant at lab strengths. Students should only use it under supervision.
- Bacteria work, even with harmless strains, falls under your institution's biosafety rules.
- Home bristlebots are safe. Just keep coin cells away from small children, because swallowed button batteries cause serious injury.
