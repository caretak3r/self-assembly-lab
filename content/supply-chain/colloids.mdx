# Colloids and nanoparticles

Colloidal crystals, nanoparticle films, gold-on-DNA arrays, and particle self-assembly movies in the digests use **particles from tens of nanometers to tens of micrometers**, suspended in liquid.

## What exists today

- **Gold and silver nanoparticles** in many sizes and coatings, sold as ready-to-use suspensions.
- **Polystyrene and silica microspheres** with tight size control, plain or fluorescent, and with chemical handles on the surface.
- **Magnetic (paramagnetic) beads** for steering particles with a magnet.
- **Janus particles** (two different halves) in larger sizes. Smaller ones are mostly made in research labs.
- **Surfactants and stabilizers** (soap-like molecules) from general chemical suppliers.

## Maturity

- Plain particles: **industrial**. They are widely used in diagnostics and coatings.
- Functionalized particles (DNA-coated, patchy, shape-controlled): **university-lab**. Usually made in-house.
- Programmable colloidal materials as products: **not yet available**.

## Bottlenecks and gaps

- **Patchy and shaped particles** (sticky spots in chosen places) are the heart of programmable colloids, and there is almost no catalog supply.
- **Batch-to-batch variation** in size and surface chemistry changes how particles assemble.
- **Seeing the result** needs microscopy, and below about 200 nm you need electron microscopes.

## What would need to exist for scale-up

Catalogs of standard patchy and DNA-coated particles, shared size and surface-chemistry standards, and inline optical checks during assembly.

## Safety notes

- Dry nanopowders can be breathed in. Buy particles already suspended in liquid when you can, and never handle dry nanopowders outside a fume hood or proper enclosure.
- NIOSH recommends caution with free, unbound nanoparticles. See [NIOSH nanotechnology guidance](https://www.cdc.gov/niosh/nano/about/index.html).
- Some solvents used with particles are flammable or toxic. Follow the Safety Data Sheet that comes with every product.
