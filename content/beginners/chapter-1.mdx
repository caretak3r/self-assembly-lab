# Chapter 1 — What self-assembly looks like

Self-assembly means: **parts + interaction rules + environment → ordered structure**, without a person placing every piece by hand.

## Everyday analogies

You already know versions of this idea:

| Everyday thing | Parts | Rules / environment | Ordered result |
|----------------|-------|---------------------|----------------|
| Snowflakes | Water molecules | Freezing air, crystal growth rules | Six-sided ice crystal |
| Cereal clumps in milk | Floating rings | Capillary “Cheerios effect” | Clusters at the surface |
| Soap bubbles packing | Bubbles | Surface tension + geometry | Foam / honeycomb-like rafts |
| Magnets snapping | Magnets | North–south attraction | Chains / lattices |
| Birds flocking | Birds | Local neighbor rules | Patterns in motion |

</Callout>

## Real engineered demos (not science fiction)

Three landmark *visible* demos you will meet again:

1. **DNA origami** — a long DNA strand folds into a designed shape with help from many short “staple” strands ([Rothemund 2006](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature04586)).
2. **Kilobot swarm** — about a thousand simple robots form shapes using local communication ([Rubenstein et al. 2014](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1254295)).
3. **Modular robots** — cubes/modules that dock and reconfigure (lab prototypes, not store-bought programmable matter).

## Try this (5 minutes)

1. Pour cereal (or paper circles) onto water in a dish.
2. Gently agitate once, then wait.
3. Sketch: where do clumps form? Edge? Center?
4. Write one sentence: *What was the “rule”? What was the “environment”?*

Safety: kitchen water only — no chemicals.

## What this chapter is *not* saying

It is **not** saying you can buy programmable matter off a shelf. It is saying: the *idea* is visible in nature, breakfast physics, DNA nanotech demos, and robot labs.
