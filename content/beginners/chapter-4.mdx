# Chapter 4 — Robot swarms & modular robots you can see

If DNA is hard to see, **robots** make self-assembly visible. Same big idea: local rules + many units → global shape or motion.

## Two families

| Family | What it is | Famous example |
|--------|------------|----------------|
| **Swarm robots** | Many simple mobile robots | Kilobots (~1,024 forming shapes) |
| **Modular / reconfigurable robots** | Dockable modules that change shape | M-Blocks, SMORES, Claytronics vision |

Programmable matter sits in the **vision** column: ensembles of tiny modules that reshape on demand. Hardware today is still research-scale.

## What beginners can actually build

You probably will **not** fab a thousand Kilobots this month. Realistic starter paths:

1. **Simulate** swarms (NetLogo, Python) — Experiment 5 on [Experiments](/experiments).
2. **Few-unit modular toys** — 3D-printed blocks + magnets (see [Manufacturing](/manufacturing)).
3. **Open educational platforms** — look for university swarm courses, K-Team/Kilobot academic kits when available, or DIY vibration-robot tutorials at makerspaces.
4. **Microcontrollers** — Arduino / ESP32 for neighbor messaging prototypes (UART/IR/radio).

## Try this (human swarm)

1. Give 6 friends one rule: “Stay an arm’s length from the nearest two people; slowly form a circle.”
2. No leader talking.
3. Time how long it takes; then change the rule to “form a line.”
4. Discuss: which failures look like robot defects?

## Honesty checkpoint

Swarm demos are thrilling. Scaling to useful outdoor work, strong structures, or consumer products still hits energy, reliability, and manufacturing walls. Treat viral videos as **existence proofs**, not shipping dates.
